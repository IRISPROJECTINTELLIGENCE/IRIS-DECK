# Market evidence

The dated facts behind slides 3, 5 and 10, in the order the deck uses them, with the caveats the deck carries. URLs are in `source-register.md`. Nothing here is a forecast; where a source is secondary the row says "reported".

## 1. The insolvency base rate

- FY2025–26: 3,472 construction companies of 14,153 entering external administration, 24.5% (ASIC series 1, published 14 Sep 2026). Prior years: 24.4% (FY25), 26.9% (FY24), 27.9% (FY23), 26.1% (FY22).
- July–August 2026: construction share 37.4%, or 23.5% excluding the 540 Bathla entities. The deck quotes both so the Bathla effect is visible.
- Caveat on the deck: most failures are small builders; the lender problem is the funded jobs among them.

## 2. Bathla, August–October 2026

- 25 Aug 2026: 540 entities of the group entered voluntary administration (one administrator across all of them).
- Debt: about $3.08bn to secured lenders within about $3.4bn of creditor debt (plus about $145M ATO, about $130M unsecured), per the administrator's updates reported 25 Sep. The AFR's earlier $3.3bn total is used on slides 2 and 10; slide 3 uses the secured figure.
- About 40 private credit funds exposed (Bloomberg, reported).
- Preliminary portfolio: about 219 projects, about $3.13bn of debt against an estimated $4.87bn of value, subject to review; about $736M of inter-company receivables and payables may be overstated and were still being reconciled a month in.
- Emergency lender funding ($3–5M from five lenders) ran out in September; all 13 remaining active sites closed on 23 Sep; more than 325 of about 350 staff stood down; the court extended the convening period to 13 Sep 2027.
- Redemptions: Centuria Bass paused two funds on 14 Aug (before the administration); CVS Lane and MA Financial limited withdrawals in late August; one lender sold a senior loan and a receiver was appointed to settle presales on a near-complete project (reported 17 Oct).
- Before the failure: a decade-long lender refinanced out between late 2024 and January 2026; about 12 other lenders refinanced the projects (reported).

## 3. Regulatory response

- ASIC REP 814 (Sep 2025): commissioned research on private credit. REP 820 and REP 823 (Nov 2025): thematic surveillance of 28 funds; four of 28 disclosed borrower interest rates; fewer than half had written credit or impairment policies; ten principles published.
- Before 30 Jun 2026: snap reviews of 52 funds overseeing $76bn, with a public warning that year-end valuations must be "current, accurate and grounded in realistic assumptions".
- 22 Sep 2026: Commissioner Simone Constant, CAFBA summit, "prepare for enforcement action".
- 30 Sep 2026: ASIC statement that "current conditions are testing private credit markets", citing higher rates, refinancing pressure and residential construction.
- 28 Sep 2026: three ASX-listed credit funds halted before the open after about $168M (4.7% of $3.55bn combined NAV) was written down and the auditor could not sign by 30 Sep; the responsible entity suspended redemptions on 30 Sep; quotation reinstated in the first week of October (CFS notice, 7 Oct). The deck makes no claim about audit status.
- 22 Oct 2026: a $2.3bn secured loan fund suspended redemptions and a second fund was wound up, citing residential construction and development conditions.
- June 2026 ASIC notice on reassessing loan values before formal default; industry standard from 1 Jul 2027 requiring quarterly independent valuation (reported; references to be added).

## 4. Rates and housing

- Cash rate: 3.60% at the start of 2026; four increases to 4.60% on 29–30 Sep 2026 (unanimous). Next decision 3 Nov 2026; September-quarter CPI published 28 Oct 2026 and not assumed on the deck.
- Housing: national dwelling values −1.1% in September, sixth monthly fall, 5.2% below the March 2026 peak; sales in the past three months 19.1% below a year earlier; median days to sell in the capitals 39 (Cotality, reported).
- RBA board: housing prices falling in most capitals; new housing loans down noticeably.

## 5. Lending terms (slide 5)

- Stamford Capital Real Estate Debt Capital Markets Survey 2026 (Mar–Apr 2026; banks, second-tier banks, non-banks, private lenders): 37% of construction lenders require zero presales, up from 29% (2025) and 18% (2023); the 60–100% presale bracket fell from about 45% of lenders (2021) to 8.1%; 94.5% plan to grow loan books; 62% expect major banks to increase construction lending. A survey sample, not the market.
- Non-bank share of commercial real estate debt about 10% → 16%, 2020–24 (Foresight Analytics for Payton Capital, 2024). RBA FSR March 2026 notes modestly easier terms for developers.

## 6. The counterargument the deck carries

- Capital has not left: $1.35bn of new commercial real estate lending mandates from North American and Asian investors announced 26 Oct 2026; the market is about $213bn, up from $35bn a decade ago, and new entrants continue to arrive (Clayton Utz, 1 Oct). The deck's reading: the money is still being lent; the conditions on it are tightening, and that is the demand IRIS serves.
