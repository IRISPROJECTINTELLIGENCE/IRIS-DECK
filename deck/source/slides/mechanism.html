Left column: what a lender's QS progress drawdown report covers. A typical report has a development cost table (budget, cost to date, cost to complete), work in place valued against the builder's claim, programme and extensions of time, cost to complete on the contract sum, variations as submitted, approvals and permits, insurances, the builder's statutory declaration that subcontractors are paid, risk items and a cash flow forecast. Reports commonly state that the information supplied was not independently verified, and they make no allowance for rectifying defective work. Right column: the position organised by fundamental cause, so each item grows from a mechanism rather than an example. 1. Entitlement at the time bar: delay notices, variations, latent conditions and provisional-sum adjustments each carry a notice period; what is approaching or past its bar decides whether the cost is recoverable or the builder's own, with liquidated damages on top. 2. Forecast movement not yet notified: the records show delays, changes, latent conditions and allowance movements coming before any notice is lodged, and each carries consequential cost, most visibly preliminaries that extend with the delay. 3. Contingency and liquid allowances: a large part of forecast final cost sits as builder contingency inside trade budgets, and latent-condition and provisional-sum allowances are liquid by nature; how much of the forecast is still soft is a risk measure in itself. 4. Market exposure in the trades: rates locked at tender are open market risk once labour and material prices move; trades let against allowances and scope still to let are the price risk already crystallising or still open; the labour market moving, for example to government work, and a subcontractor that leaves for better pay leaves the builder short of labour and the programme in delay; trades owed against what has been claimed is the cash signal. 5. Completion risk: labour numbers on site against the programme, and open defects, rework and non-conformances against practical completion, show whether PC will be reached and when, so the lender can adjust its facility before the date moves. 6. Validity: trades' claims, variations, delays and disputes in train are cost the builder carries before its forecast shows them. The QS report sees each of these as submitted, if at all; IRIS reads the records they come from and values and dates each one.
