The problem · why

03

The information exists. It travels by hand, through the party it reports on.

The builder’s systems

hold the records: variations, notices, trades let, claims, payments, forecast.

The builder’s monthly report

is written from those records, by the builder.

The lender’s QS

certifies the claim from a site visit and the builder’s submissions. Not independently verified.

The lender

funds the drawdown on the certificate, weeks after the records moved.

The certificate is not wrong. It is late and narrow. It does not carry notices approaching their time bar, cost moving before it is notified, contingency already spent, or trades owed more than the builder has claimed.

That last one is usually the first sign of a builder in trouble, and the lender sees it last.

Founder observation from work inside builders across arm’s-length, joint-venture and in-house delivery. Row-by-row detail in the appendix.

The chain: five systems hold the records (Procore or Aconex, Jobpac, PayApps, ProcurePro, email and the share drive); the builder's team writes the monthly report from them (the internal cost report and the PCG report to the client); the lender's quantity surveyor values work in place from a site inspection and checks the claim against the contract sum, working from the claim, the variation and EOT registers as submitted, the statutory declaration that trades are paid and the builder's programme, and reports that the information was not independently verified and that no allowance is made for defects; the lender funds the drawdown on that certificate. What sits beneath it, by cause: entitlement at the time bar; forecast movement not yet notified and the prelims it extends; how much of forecast final cost is still contingency and provisional sums; market exposure in the trades; completion risk; and cash strain in the trades, what subcontractors are owed against what the builder has claimed and been paid, usually the first sign of a builder in trouble. Since 2021, fixed prices, thinner builders and in-house delivery have moved more of the risk inside the project while the lender's view stayed a certificate on the head contract (appendix A3).
