Problem · the mechanism

04

The position sits in five systems and travels up by hand.
Each hand-off filters it. The lender sees it last.

1 · The project’s systems

Procore or Aconex, Jobpac, PayApps, ProcurePro and the inbox. Variations, delays, trades let and money owed each live somewhere different.

2 · The builder’s monthly report

Assembled by hand from those systems, by the party it reports on.

3 · The lender’s QS certificate

Work in place checked against the claim, from a site visit and the builder’s submissions. Not independently verified; no allowance for defects.

4 · The facility decision

The drawdown is funded on the certificate, weeks after the records moved. Finish, sell or write down is decided on the same summary.

What the certificate does not carry: notices against time bars, cost moving before it is notified, contingency already spent, market exposure in the trades, defects, and trades owed more than the builder has claimed. The last is usually the first sign.

Since 2021, fixed prices, thinner builders and in-house delivery have moved more of the risk inside the project. The lender’s view has not moved.

Founder observation, from work inside builders across three delivery models. Detail: appendix A2, A3.

The chain: five systems hold the records; the builder's team assembles the monthly report from them (internal cost report and the PCG report to the client); the lender's quantity surveyor values work in place from a site inspection and checks the claim against the contract sum, working from the claim, the variation and EOT registers as submitted, the statutory declaration that trades are paid and the builder's programme, and reports that the information was not independently verified and that no allowance is made for defects; the lender funds the drawdown on that certificate. What sits beneath it, by cause: entitlement at the time bar (notices, variations, latent conditions near or past the deadline that decides who pays); forecast movement not yet notified and the prelims it extends; how much of forecast final cost is still contingency and provisional sums; market exposure in the trades (rates locked while prices move, scope unlet, trades let against allowances); completion risk (labour on site against programme, open defects and non-conformances against PC); and cash strain in the trades, what subcontractors are owed against what the builder has claimed and been paid, which is usually the first sign of a builder in trouble and the one the lender sees last. Appendix A2 carries the row-by-row table with the QS item each sits beneath and the source system; A3 the four market shifts since 2021 with sources (Stamford Capital 2026 survey; builder failures 2022–25; non-bank share of commercial property debt).
