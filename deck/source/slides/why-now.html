The market moved to where the position matters most.
Capital is still being deployed. It is asking harder questions.

The reason · October 2026

Rates and feasibility. The cash rate rose 100 bps in 2026, to 4.60% on 30 September; annual CPI 4.3% to September, trimmed mean 3.6%. Dwelling values 5.2% below their March peak; sales 19% below a year earlier (RBA; ABS; Cotality).

Bathla. 540 entities into administration on 25 August; about $3.3bn owed to about 40 funds. Redemptions paused from 14 August; sites closed, receivers appointed from late September (reported).

Valuation scrutiny. ASIC reviewed 52 funds holding $76bn before 30 June, warned on 22 September to prepare for enforcement and on 30 September named residential construction. Three listed credit funds suspended 28 September after a $168M write-down (ASIC; ASX; reported).

Not confined to one group. A $2.3bn real-estate credit fund suspended redemptions on 22 October. Construction was 37% of insolvencies in July–August with Bathla, 23.5% without (ASIC; reported).

The enabler · 2024

Models that read contracts, correspondence and cost records together and cite the source for each figure, at a cost that works per project per month. The tooling makes IRIS possible; the market makes it wanted.

Across the cycle

The layer beneath the certificate goes unreported in good markets too; a downturn raises the cost of not knowing. Monitoring written into facilities is paid on what is lent, so it grows with lending rather than with distress, and the data it builds is the standard the next cycle is priced on.

In June 2026 ASIC told private credit funds to reassess loan values before formal default; the industry standard from July 2027 requires quarterly independent valuation. Forecasts of further distress are not treated as fact.
