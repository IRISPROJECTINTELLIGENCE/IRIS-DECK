In 2026 the market moved to where the position matters most.
Capital is still being deployed. It is asking harder questions.

The reason · October 2026

Rates and feasibility. The cash rate rose 100 bps in 2026, to 4.60% on 30 September. Dwelling values are 5.2% below their March peak; sales 19% below a year earlier (RBA; Cotality, September).

Bathla. 540 entities into administration on 25 August; about $3.3bn owed; about 40 private credit funds exposed. Redemptions paused at several funds from 14 August; sites closed and receivers appointed from late September (reported).

Valuation scrutiny. ASIC reviewed 52 funds holding $76bn before 30 June, warned on 22 September to prepare for enforcement and on 30 September named residential construction. Three listed credit funds suspended 28 September after a $168M write-down (ASIC; ASX; reported).

Spreading, not forecast. A $2.3bn real-estate credit fund suspended redemptions on 22 October. Construction was 37% of insolvencies in July–August with Bathla, 23.5% without (ASIC; reported).

The enabler · 2024

Models that read contracts, correspondence and cost records together and cite the source for each figure, at a cost that works per project per month. Necessary, not sufficient: the tooling makes IRIS possible; the market makes it wanted.

The counterargument

Capital has not left. $1.35bn of new commercial real-estate lending mandates were announced on 26 October; the market is about $213bn and new entrants keep arriving (reported). The money is still being lent; the conditions on it are tightening, and that is the demand IRIS serves.

In June 2026 ASIC told private credit funds to reassess loan values before formal default; the industry standard from July 2027 requires quarterly independent valuation (reported). Forecasts of further distress are not treated as fact; the September-quarter CPI is due 28 October and not assumed.
