Solution · distressed-project assessments

A funded project stalls. The first task is to find its position.
IRIS finds it in the records.

Proposed functionality, at a separate fee, once the platform is proven on live jobs. No engagement has been run.

1 · Trigger

A facility out of balance, a builder default or administration, a receiver appointed, a refinance or restructure under review.

2 · Buyer

The lender, its receiver or administrator, or the capital partner. Paid per engagement, with the other recovery costs.

3 · Access, lawfully

Before an appointment, the facility’s information rights and the builder’s side deed. After, the receiver’s or administrator’s agreement.

4 · Ingest

Cost ledger, commitments, claims, variations, notices, programme, correspondence; the QS pack beside them.

5 · Outputs

Where the project stands across the eight domains; each finding valued, dated and linked to its record; a register of gaps.

6 · Decisions

The lender’s call to keep or replace the builder, fund, refinance, sell or enforce, made on the position. IRIS advises nothing.

Today this takes weeks of collating records while the site stalls and interest runs. IRIS gives a complete, source-linked position in days, with a trail a committee, a court or a buyer can check.

Sequence: builder pilots prove the position, a lender design partner shapes the view, monitoring under a mandate scales the product; distressed-project assessments are part of that scaling, priced per engagement. Before distress, the same position serves routine lender decisions (appendix A2).

Proposed, not demonstrated, and part of the scale stage: the platform is proven on builder pilots and shaped with a lender design partner first, then written into facilities at scale; distressed-project assessments are offered as part of that scaling, at a separate per-engagement fee, because they are where the information problem is most expensive and where a lender already pays outside advisers, so a budget exists. The first assessment is a controlled one on a historic record set, scored against the outcome reached, before any live engagement. Access, the lawful path in plain terms. The lender has no contract with the builder unless one is signed, so two documents do the work. The facility agreement binds the developer (the borrower): it must give the lender project information and procure its builder's cooperation. The builder's side deed (also called a tripartite deed or builder's deed of covenant), signed by lender, developer and builder at financial close, is the builder's direct promise to the lender: it acknowledges the lender's security, agrees to keep performing if the lender steps in, and gives the lender and its QS information and access; lenders already require it as standard. An IRIS clause sits there: read-only API access to that project's systems, for monitoring, for that project. After an appointment the picture changes: a receiver appointed over the developer takes the developer's rights under the head contract and can give consent; if the builder itself is in administration, its administrator controls its books and software subscriptions, so access needs the administrator's agreement and is not automatic. Outside distress the developer, as the builder's client, can request information under the head contract and consent to sharing it with its lender. The developer's own records and the lender's QS pack are available regardless. Two limits: the builder's duty must stand on its own and not be a precondition to its payment claims (BRB Modular v AWX [2015] QSC 218), and subcontract confidentiality terms decide what trade data can be shown. To be confirmed with lawyers (the month-9 legal opinion); not legal advice. What the recovery assessment does not do: advise on the decision, value the asset, certify cost to complete, or assess the builder's solvency; the QS, the valuer and the insolvency practitioner stay. What it gives the decision is the collated position, the project context behind it and the gaps in the existing position. On the replacement-builder point (slide 3): an incoming builder will not take liability for the fallen builder's work, so it prices the risk of reworking what was done, the open quality items and forecast exposures that could affect its ability to complete with the indemnity it wants, and the completeness of the records it inherits; a source-linked position with gaps listed narrows what it has to price blind. Speed: the design aim is a first position in days from access, not hours, and the time depends on how complete and well-kept the records are; where they are thin, the output is a register of gaps rather than a filled-in number. Pricing is a hypothesis: a fixed fee per engagement, not a share of recovery, to avoid a conflict between the position reported and the fee earned.
