A funded project stalls. The first task is to find its position.
IRIS finds it in the records, not in each party’s version.

Proposed functionality, at a separate fee, once the platform is proven on live jobs. No engagement has been run.

1 · Trigger

A missed in-balance test, a builder default or administration, a receiver appointed, a refinance or restructure under review.

2 · Buyer

The lender, its receiver or administrator, or the capital partner. Paid per engagement, with the other recovery costs.

3 · Access, lawfully

Before an appointment: the facility’s information rights and the builder’s side deed to the lender. After: the receiver’s or administrator’s agreement; or the developer’s consent.

4 · Ingest

The job’s source system data: cost ledger, commitments, claims, variations, notices, programme, correspondence; the QS pack beside them.

5 · Outputs

The project’s risk position across the domains: cost, time, scope, procurement, payment, quality, safety, compliance; each finding valued, dated and source-linked; a register of gaps.

6 · Decisions

What the position supports: keep the builder or replace it; fund to completion, refinance or restructure; sell or enforce. It shows what a replacement builder would be pricing in.

Today the cost is weeks of collating and reconciling records while the site stalls, interest runs and buyers near sunset dates. The value is a complete, source-linked position sooner, with an evidence trail that a committee, a court or a buyer can check. Gaps are listed, not filled in.

Sequence: builder pilots prove the position, a lender design partner shapes the view, monitoring projects through a mandate scales it; assessments of distressed projects are conducted alongside, priced per engagement. Before distress, the same position serves routine lender decisions (A2).

Proposed, not demonstrated, and deliberately last in the sequence: the platform is proven on builder pilots and shaped with a lender design partner first, then written into facilities at scale; distressed-project assessments are offered after that, at a separate per-engagement fee, because they are where the information problem is most expensive and where a lender already pays outside advisers, so a budget exists. The first assessment is a controlled one on a historic record set, scored against the outcome reached, before any live engagement. Access, the lawful path in plain terms. The lender has no contract with the builder unless one is signed, so two documents do the work. The facility agreement binds the developer (the borrower): it must give the lender project information and procure its builder's cooperation. The builder's side deed (also called a tripartite deed or builder's deed of covenant), signed by lender, developer and builder at financial close, is the builder's direct promise to the lender: it acknowledges the lender's security, agrees to keep performing if the lender steps in, and gives the lender and its QS information and access; lenders already require it as standard. An IRIS clause sits there: read-only API access to that project's systems, for monitoring, for that project. After an appointment the picture changes: a receiver appointed over the developer takes the developer's rights under the head contract and can give consent; if the builder itself is in administration, its administrator controls its books and software subscriptions, so access needs the administrator's agreement and is not automatic. Outside distress the developer, as the builder's client, can request information under the head contract and consent to sharing it with its lender. The developer's own records and the lender's QS pack are available regardless. Two limits: the builder's duty must stand on its own and not be a precondition to its payment claims (BRB Modular v AWX [2015] QSC 218), and subcontract confidentiality terms decide what trade data can be shown. To be confirmed with lawyers (the month-9 legal opinion); not legal advice. What the recovery assessment does not do: value the asset, certify cost to complete, or assess the builder's solvency; the QS, the valuer and the insolvency practitioner stay. Speed: the design aim is a first position in days from access, not hours, and the time depends on how complete and well-kept the records are; where they are thin, the output is a register of gaps rather than a filled-in number. Pricing is a hypothesis: a fixed fee per engagement, not a share of recovery, to avoid a conflict between the position reported and the fee earned.
