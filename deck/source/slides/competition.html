In October 2026 we found no software that reconciles a project’s systems into one position for builder, developer and lender. Lenders’ controls today are the QS certificate and, on some jobs, a payment-account control; IRIS sits beside both and reads the records beneath them (A7).

13

The claim is bounded: as of October 2026, after a review of about sixty products and firms across eight categories (builder platforms, builder ERP and cost systems, trade payment and compliance, lender-side draw and monitoring tools, owner project controls, AI on construction records, credit data, and the service incumbents), from public sources with vendor claims marked, we found no software that reads across a project's builder systems and reports one reconciled, source-linked position to the builder, the developer and the lender. What lenders write into facilities today: the QS certificate, on every facility; and on some jobs a payment-account control, IPEX, which reconciles certified progress against money paid out of a project bank account and whose chief executive said in November 2025 that some lenders mandate it as a condition of lending (a vendor claim; no lender has confirmed it publicly). IPEX reads a bank account and payment files, not the builder's systems; it sees no internal cost, commitments against allowances, unsubmitted variations, notices or time bars. It is the name an Australian non-bank lender is most likely to raise, and IRIS sits beside it. Built Technologies is the US proof that lenders pay for a platform that reads Procore financials (300-plus lenders; its Procore sync covers commitments, pay apps and direct costs); it stops at the draw layer and has no Australian presence; Rabbet and Land Gorilla are the same layer. Procore (Datagrid, closed January 2026; owner tools with funding-source tracking due late 2026) and Trimble (Document Crunch, April 2026; owner of Jobpac) now own cross-system AI for the builder; neither has a lender view, and each answers to the builder that pays. Mastt, Kahua and Northspyre are owner ledgers with no builder-system integration. Octant, Alloovium, Slate and Trunk Tools read one builder's records for that builder. CreditorWatch and Equifax iCIRT rate the builder as a company. QS firms and restructuring advisers (KordaMentha, McGrathNicol, FTI) rebuild a position by hand and have no software product; IRIS feeds that work. The defensible difference is lender-side reconciliation across systems, permissioning and reliance terms, and a condition in the facility, all unproven today and what the round tests. We would partner with a product similar enough in logic to merge with; none found. The weakest point: lenders already hold two controls at the drawdown that expose less builder data than IRIS needs, so IRIS has to show that the exposure in the records before it reaches a claim or a payment is worth a third control, and that builders will grant read access to get the job.
