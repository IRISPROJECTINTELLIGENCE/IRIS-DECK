What holds the condition

Is already under contract

On a job under way the builder owes nothing new. The first conditions go on new facilities and at extensions or refinances, when the loan is re-papered and the side deed is signed.

Won’t bid, or carves it out

It’s worth having: the builder gets IRIS’s view of its own job. We expect a carve-out to read as a signal to the lender, and opting out to cost more as lenders adopt; both are untested until a lender does.

Disputes a finding

Each figure links to its record. The builder sees a finding first and can annotate it before the lender reads it, and the position is checked against data the builder does not write: subcontractor claims, the QS’s assessment.

Worries others will see its margins

Role-based permissions: builder, developer and lender each get their own view. Commercially sensitive items stay with the builder; each party sees the risk position that concerns it.

The facility agreement binds the borrower. The builder is bound by what it signs: the tender and award conditions and the head contract, then the side deed at financial close, which lenders already require as standard practice. The builder's duty stands on its own and is not a condition of the builder's payment claims, because a contractual precondition to payment claims was held void in BRB Modular v AWX [2015] QSC 218. On jobs already under way the builder owes nothing new, so first conditions go on new facilities. Once signed, refusing access is a breach. The drawdown condition is met by an exported pack that records it, so a builder cannot hold the developer's drawdown hostage. Each lender adopts the condition on its own terms. To be confirmed with lawyers; not legal advice. Adoption: where builders have plenty of work, the condition has to be worth having, not just enforceable. Builders take up IRIS first to run their own jobs, so a lender's condition asks them to share a view they already use. Cursor spread the same way: developers adopted it, then pushed their employers into team contracts (Sacra). Legitimate reasons to refuse are removed up front: nothing written back, the builder sees findings before the lender, the funded project and nothing beyond it, and role-based permissions that keep commercially sensitive items with the builder. The lender sees whether the job is forecast to lose money, and by how much, not the builder's rates or subcontract prices. What a carve-out leaves after that is, we expect, a signal; that and the network effect of several lenders adopting are hypotheses until a lender adopts, and the slide now says so. Several lenders requiring the same dashboard, published as a standard, leave fewer jobs to opt out to; MCP became the standard for connecting AI tools once rival AI companies adopted it (Anthropic, December 2025).
