A3

Backs slide 4’s closing line: since 2021, fixed prices, thinner builders and a trend of in-house delivery have moved more of the risk inside the project. Fixed prices: Probuild, Condev, PBS, Porter Davis, St Hilliers and Roberts Co failed between 2022 and 2025 (reported; the timeline is on slide 3). Thinner builders: construction was 26.1% of companies entering external administration in FY2022, 27.9% in FY2023, 26.9% in FY2024, 24.4% in FY2025 and 24.5% in FY2025-26 (ASIC series 1, 14 September 2026). In-house delivery: where builder and developer are related entities or co-investors, the builder’s margin and the developer’s equity are the same group capital, so a project loss has no second balance sheet to land on and the builder tends to fail with the developer. Bathla’s in-house builder entered administration with the group on 25 August 2026. The share of jobs delivered in-house or by joint venture is a founder observation and has not been measured. Under the builder’s side deed a lender can take the developer’s place if the developer defaults; where the builder has failed too, lenders appoint receivers and fund a replacement builder. Presales: Stamford Capital’s Real Estate Debt Capital Markets Survey, run in March and April 2026, found 37% of construction lenders requiring no presales, up from 29% in 2025 and 18% in 2023, while the share requiring 60 to 100% presales fell from about 45% in 2021 to 8%. Lending has tightened since August 2026 (slide 8). Many jobs still run at arm’s length on a fixed price; the four changes are observed developments, with sources on A7.
