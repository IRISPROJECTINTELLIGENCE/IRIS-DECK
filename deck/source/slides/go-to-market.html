Route to market

10

Builders prove it on live jobs. A lender writes it into a facility.

1 · Builders

Two pilots on live jobs. The position must reconcile to the builder’s own cost reports before anyone pays. Then a monthly fee per live job that follows the builder across its jobs.

2 · A design-partner lender

A non-bank private credit fund shapes the lender view from the pilots and runs it beside its QS on a live facility. One such fund has told us the lender view would be useful to it.

3 · The condition

A credit committee accepts a live result and writes IRIS into one facility as a drawdown condition. Then its book. Then the next lender.

How the condition holds. The facility binds the developer. The head contract and side deed bind the builder, from the next facility, extension or refinance. A builder under contract today owes nothing new.

Nothing is assumed automatic: no access, no pilot; no reconciled result, no lender test; no live result, no condition. Mandate mechanics in the appendix.

Sequence (D-07): builder pilots are the technical and commercial proving ground; the lender design partner (target: a non-bank private credit fund) shapes the lender view from those pilots with the pilot builder's consent; at scale the lender requires IRIS as a drawdown condition on the whole project debt stack, the developer pays in basis points as it pays the QS, and the builder opens its systems under the head contract and side deed. The lender signal on the slide is a founder-reported conversation with one non-bank private credit fund, unnamed; the first ask is a design-partner letter and one facility to run beside its QS. Mandate mechanics: lender to developer by the facility agreement; developer to bidders by a tender or award condition; developer to builder by a head-contract clause; all three by the side deed at financial close (appendix A4). A builder that carves it out gets IRIS's view of its own job; we expect a carve-out to read as a signal to the lender, untested until a lender adopts.
