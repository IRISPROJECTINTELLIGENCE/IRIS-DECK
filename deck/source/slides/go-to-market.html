Builders prove it. A lender shapes it. The mandate scales it.

Now · Builders

Two pilots on live jobs, then a monthly fee per live job that follows the builder across its jobs. Gate: the position reconciles to the builder’s own cost reports.

Builder · $3,500 a month per job

Alongside · A lender

A non-bank lender shapes the lender view from the pilots and uses it on a live facility beside its QS. Gate: security, PI and reliance terms in place.

No fee · the partner’s time

Scale · The mandate

A credit committee accepts a live result and writes IRIS into one facility as a drawdown condition; then its book; then the next lender.

Developer · 15 bps on the facility

How the mandate holds. The facility binds the developer; the head contract and side deed bind the builder, from the next facility, extension or refinance. A builder under contract today owes nothing new.

Nothing assumed automatic: no access, no pilot; no reconciled result, no lender test; no live result, no condition. Mandate mechanics: appendix A4.

Three workstreams on one platform, in sequence (D-07): builder pilots are the technical and commercial proving ground and the wedge (D-33: the subscription follows the builder across its live jobs); the lender design partner (target: a non-bank private credit fund) shapes the lender view from those pilots with the pilot builder's consent; at scale the lender requires IRIS as a drawdown condition on the whole project debt stack, the developer pays in basis points as it pays the QS, and the builder opens its systems under the head contract and side deed. Distressed-project assessments are part of the scale stage, priced per engagement. Mandate mechanics: lender to developer by the facility agreement; developer to all bidders by a tender or award condition; developer to builder by a head-contract clause; all three parties by the side deed at financial close. The builder opens its systems read-only, sees each finding first, and builder, developer and lender each get their own view. A builder that carves it out gets IRIS's view of its own job; we expect a carve-out to read as a signal to the lender, which is untested until a lender adopts.
