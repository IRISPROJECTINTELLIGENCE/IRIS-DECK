Why we expect each party accepts it

The lender sees time, cost and quality risk positions as they move.

The developer meets a condition of its debt, as with the QS report today, and may borrow at a lower rate from a lender that sees inside the project.

The builder accepts it to bid, and sees each finding first.

Three applications of one platform, not three businesses, in the order they can be proven. Builders first because they run the systems and carry the cost risk; their pilots test the position against their own cost reports and are the technical proving ground; the first builder is secured as an R&D partner with access to real project data. The lender design partner works alongside from the first pilots, deciding what the lender view shows and how it is structured from the lender's perspective, so the lender can read the project's risk against its facility and position; the view is shaped on real pilot data rather than a mock-up. Monitoring at scale needs the reconciliation evidence, security certification, professional indemnity and reliance terms, and a lender willing to put a condition in its documents; the first condition goes on a new facility or at an extension or refinance, when the loan is re-papered. Distressed-project assessments are part of the scale stage, as a separate per-engagement fee on the same position (slide 10), once the platform has proven itself on live jobs; they are not the entry point (owner decision O-09, 30 October). Who requires, pays and opens the systems, stated once: in the pilots no one requires IRIS, the builder chooses it, pays for it and opens its own systems read-only; the lender view is shaped on pilot data solely with the pilot builder's consent, or on a job the design partner itself funds; at scale the lender requires it as a drawdown condition, the developer pays in basis points on the facility as it pays the QS, and the builder opens its systems under the head contract and side deed (slide 13). The fee is 15 bps a year of the loan amount: the full project debt, senior alone or senior with mezzanine, with a floor of $3,500 a month and no cap; on the base case's $80M facility that is $120,000 a year (D-50, D-51). The party that pays at scale is not the party that opens the systems, and the deck says so rather than leaving an investment committee to find it. Why builders say yes: their own job in one view from systems they already run, rates and margins stay with them. Why lenders say yes: the position beneath the certificate at each drawdown, on one standard across the book. Why developers pay: changes priced before approval, the overrun known earlier, paid like the QS fee. Not in the numbers: across enough facilities the data becomes a pricing signal for construction risk. Precedent for a lender-imposed control beyond the QS: IPEX's chief executive said in November 2025 that some lenders make its payment controls a condition of lending on certain jobs (vendor claim).
