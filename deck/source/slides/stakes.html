03

Construction failures put billions of lender capital at risk.
Early visibility at depth is what protects that capital.

The lender carries the full facility, and it takes a finished project to repay it. Until then its view arrives monthly, in reports built retrospectively from what the builder submits. Bathla’s secured lenders were owed $3.08bn on that view.

While the project runs

Monthly

How a lender sees a running project

Today: each month the lender reads the QS report and the builder’s own PCG report. The QS records what has been built and paid for. Where the job is heading, and what it will cost to finish, rests on the builder’s word.

IRIS: the actuals and the forecast together. Cost to complete, completion date, unpriced variations, unresolved time claims and trade risk (who is owed what, whose labour is drifting off the job), as the records change.

When it fails

12 lenders

Took on Bathla projects in its last two years

Today: Bathla’s lender of a decade refinanced out between late 2024 and January 2026, and about 12 others took its projects on. The group failed in August. A replacement builder then prices what it cannot see: in our experience, 20–40% on the remaining works.

IRIS: the project’s position on day one, record by record, so the lender can size the recovery and handover to a replacement builder in days.

Bathla figures from the administrator's updates as reported by The Urban Developer on 25 September 2026: about $3.08bn owed to secured lenders within about $3.4bn of total creditor debt, including about $145m to the ATO and $130m to unsecured creditors; a preliminary portfolio of about 219 projects carrying about $3.13bn of debt against an estimated $4.87bn of value, subject to review; and about $736m of inter-company receivables and payables that may be overstated and were still being reconciled. These are exposures, sums owed; the lenders' losses will be set as receivers sell, and the write-downs recognised so far are on A7. All 13 remaining active sites were closed on 23 September after emergency lender funding of $3m to $5m from five lenders ran out; the NSW Supreme Court extended the convening period to 13 September 2027. One lender's senior loan on a near-complete project was later sold and a receiver appointed to settle presales (ABC News, 17 October 2026). The $3.3bn figure used elsewhere in the deck is the AFR's total; this slide uses the secured-lender figure. Right column: a lender with a decade-long Bathla relationship refinanced out between late 2024 and January 2026, and about 12 other lenders refinanced the projects (The Urban Developer, 25 August 2026); the group entered administration on 25 August 2026, seven months after that exit. The point to make aloud is that each lender assessed the projects with the records it had; the gap is in what those records could show, which is the next slide. The replacement-builder premium of 20 to 40 per cent is founder experience, not a measured figure; part of it prices what the incoming builder cannot see in the records. ASIC's REP 820 found most private credit funds lacked effective separation between investment decisions and the monitoring of loan value.
