Problem

03

Construction failures put billions of lender capital at risk.
Early visibility at depth is what protects that capital.

The lender carries the full facility, and it takes a finished project to repay it. Until then its view arrives monthly, in reports built retrospectively from what the builder submits. Bathla’s secured lenders held $3.08bn on that view.

While the project runs

Monthly

How a lender sees a running project

Today: each month the lender reads the QS report and the builder’s own report. The QS records what has been built and paid for. Where the job is heading, and what it will cost to finish, rests on the builder’s word.

IRIS: the actuals and the forecast together. Cost to complete, completion date, unpriced variations, unresolved time claims and trade risk (who is owed what, whose labour is drifting off the job), as the records change.

When it fails

8 months

From a lender’s low-to-medium risk rating to failure

Today: a Bathla project was rated low to medium risk at 83% complete in December 2025; the group failed in August 2026. The replacement builder then prices what it cannot see, 20–40% on the remaining works.

IRIS: the project’s position on day one, record by record, so the lender can size the recovery and handover to a replacement builder in days.

Sources: The Urban Developer, 25 August and 25 September 2026; Rampart, 6 September 2026; founder observations. Detail in appendix A7.

Bathla figures from the administrator's updates as reported by The Urban Developer on 25 September 2026: about $3.08bn owed to secured lenders within about $3.4bn of total creditor debt, including about $145m to the ATO and $130m to unsecured creditors; a preliminary portfolio of about 219 projects carrying about $3.13bn of debt against an estimated $4.87bn of value, subject to review; and about $736m of inter-company receivables and payables that may be overstated and were still being reconciled. All 13 remaining active sites were closed on 23 September after emergency lender funding of $3m to $5m from five lenders ran out; the NSW Supreme Court extended the convening period to 13 September 2027. One lender's senior loan on a near-complete project was later sold and a receiver appointed to settle presales (ABC News, 17 October 2026). The $3.3bn figure used elsewhere in the deck is the AFR's total; this slide uses the secured-lender figure. Construction's share of insolvencies, 24.5 per cent in FY2025-26, is on slide 2 and A6 (ASIC). The replacement-builder premium of 20 to 40 per cent is founder experience, not a measured figure; part of it prices what the incoming builder cannot see in the records. Earlier context: a lender with a decade-long Bathla relationship refinanced out between late 2024 and January 2026; about 12 other lenders refinanced the projects, and one took over a 339-apartment project at 83 per cent complete in December 2025, rating non-completion risk low to medium (The Urban Developer, 25 August 2026; Rampart, 6 September 2026). ASIC's REP 820 found most private credit funds lacked effective separation between investment decisions and the monitoring of loan value. Scope of the example: IRIS reads a project's builder systems, so on a Bathla project it would show that project's trades owed against what was claimed and certified, and its cost position against forecast; it would not show the group's inter-company position across 540 entities, which is the administrator's reconciliation. The slide is in two states, running and failed, with the lender's sight today against what IRIS shows in each. The monthly cycle on the left is the QS progress-claim report, a founder observation of standard practice. The right column is the December 2025 refinancing: a lender took over a 339-apartment Bathla project at 83 per cent complete and rated non-completion risk low to medium, about eight months before the group's administration on 25 August 2026 (The Urban Developer, 25 August 2026; Rampart, 6 September 2026). The point to make aloud is that the lender assessed it with the records it had; the gap is in what those records could show, which is the next slide.
