Simply put.

The problem

Construction produces about one in four Australian insolvencies (ASIC, FY2025–26). When a funded project gets into trouble, the first question is what its real position is, and that answer sits across fragmented records in separate systems, that is reconciled by hand, monthly. It reaches the stakeholders funding the job late, incomplete, or shaped by the party reporting it.

The product

IRIS reads the systems a project already runs, by read only API connection, and reconciles the data into one risk view of the project, by domain, with each finding linked to the record behind it. It writes nothing back and sits alongside the QS.

Two products, one platform

IRIS for Builders, now: pilot deployments on live projects at a monthly fee per live job, to prove the position and protect the builder’s margin.
IRIS for Lenders, alongside: a design-partner lender shapes the view; at scale it is written into the facility and paid by the developer in basis points. Distressed-project assessments at a separate fee.

Why now

Rates up 100 bps in 2026; a $3.3bn developer-builder failure in August, about 40 private credit funds exposed, redemptions frozen, ASIC moving to enforcement. Capital still flows, on harder questions (reported).

Where we are

MVP built on a populated Procore sandbox, shown to a South East Queensland Tier 2 builder; live-project access being negotiated. One non-bank private credit fund has told us the lender view would be useful to it. Pre-revenue.

The round

$500K SAFE at a $5M cap. It buys the evidence: a deployment-ready enterprise product, live data, reconciliation against builders’ own cost reports, a lender live beside its QS, a security review, and paid demand.
