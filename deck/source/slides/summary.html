The problem

Construction produces about one in four Australian insolvencies (ASIC, FY2025–26). A lender funds a project on what it will cost to finish. That figure in its truest form sits across the builder’s separate delivery systems, reconciled by hand, monthly, and reaches the project lender/s late, incomplete and often filtered or shaped by the party reporting it.

The product

IRIS reads the builder’s systems by read-only API and reconciles them to show where a project stands on time, cost and quality, and what is putting each at risk. Each finding traces to its record. IRIS sits beside the Quantity Surveyor.

Two products, one platform

IRIS for Builders, now: pilot deployments at a monthly fee per live job, to prove the product and protect the builder’s margin.
IRIS for Lenders, alongside: a design-partner lender shapes the view; at scale it is written into the facility and paid by the developer in basis points. Distressed-project assessments at a separate fee.

Why now

Rates up 125 bps in 2026; a $3.3bn developer-builder failure in August, about 40 private credit funds exposed, redemptions frozen, ASIC moving to enforcement. Capital still flows, just on harder questions. IRIS gives lenders the clarity and depth into delivery risk required to protect their funds.

Where we are

Proof of concept built and running on a populated Procore sandbox. We have secured an R&D partnership with a South East Queensland Tier 2 builder, with access to real project data, and are negotiating a technical partnership for the build of our enterprise-ready product. The concept has been validated by builders, including a regional commercial manager at a multinational Tier 1, and a private lender has confirmed it wants the product. We are pre-revenue.
