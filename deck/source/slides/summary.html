Simply put.

The problem

Construction produces about one in four Australian insolvencies (ASIC, FY2025–26). When a funded project gets into trouble, the first question is what its real position is, and that answer sits in records no one party holds, reconciled by hand, monthly. It reaches the people funding the job late, tainted, in part, or not at all.

The product

IRIS reads the systems a project already runs, by read only API, and reconciles all existing project data into source-linked risk positions, across domains, with drill-down functionality. It writes nothing back and sits alongside the QS.

Three uses, one platform

Now: builder pilot deployments on live projects, a monthly fee per project, to prove the position. 
Alongside: a lender design partner shaping the lender view from those pilots. 
At scale: monitoring written into the facility, paid in basis points; distressed-project assessments at a separate fee.

Why now

Rates up 100 bps in 2026; a $3.3bn developer-builder failure in August, about 40 private credit funds exposed, redemptions frozen, ASIC moving to enforcement. Capital still flows, on harder questions (reported).

Where we are

POC built on a populated Procore sandbox, shown to a South East Queensland Tier 2 builder; live-project access is being negotiated, not yet granted. Pre-revenue. No recovery engagement has been run.

The round

$750K SAFE at a $7.5M cap. It buys measurable validation: live data access, an accuracy test against builders’ own cost reports, a lender live beside its QS, security review, and evidence of paid demand.
