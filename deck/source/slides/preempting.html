Questions investors ask

Preempting questions.

Doesn’t the QS already do this?

No. The QS certifies work in place against the claim, monthly, at the head contract. IRIS reconciles the records beneath it across systems, drill-down to each record, conflicts shown. It certifies nothing; the QS stays.

Getting records on a troubled job; what if some are missing?

Via the facility’s information rights and the builder’s side deed, a receiver’s or administrator’s powers, or the developer’s consent, under confidentiality terms per engagement. Gaps and disputes stay listed as gaps.

Is this builder scoring?

No. The lender sees the project’s delivery risk position and the builder’s position on that project, and adjusts its facility. IRIS takes no view on the builder as a company.

Why would a builder give access? Can a lender require it?

The builder gets IRIS on its own job first; nothing written back, rates stay in its view. A lender requires it through the facility agreement, side deed and head contract, as it does QS access. To be confirmed with lawyers.

Are the numbers right, and who carries the risk?

Rules over the records, no model weights; each figure source-linked. Reconciled against builders’ own cost reports and three historic record sets before any lender relies on it. PI cover sized for lender use; reliance terms set with the design-partner lender.

What stops a platform, QS or restructuring firm doing this?

A platform answers to the builder that pays for it; a lender requiring one dictates the builder’s software. QS and restructuring firms inspect, certify or act; they are channels. IRIS reads across systems, which is unproven.

Why not price recovery on the outcome?

A fee tied to the recovery would give IRIS a stake in the position it reports. Recovery assessments are a fixed fee per engagement, set with the first buyer and not in the model.

Who pays, and what if adoption takes longer?

Builders $3,500 a month per project; developers 15 bps once a condition; assessments per engagement. The builder line stands on its own. If adoption slips, the round still buys the reconciliation evidence.

Order runs from product to business. QS: the lender's QS verifies physical work in place and cost to complete against the contract sum; IRIS reconciles the records beneath the head contract and sits alongside; it values nothing, inspects nothing and certifies nothing. Recovery access: on a live job, facility-agreement information undertakings and the builder's side deed carry access; after an appointment the administrator or receiver controls the books and the builder's software subscriptions must be kept alive, so their agreement is needed; the developer's records and the lender's QS pack are available regardless; each engagement runs under its own confidentiality terms; gaps and disputes are reported as such. Scope: the lender sees the project's risk position and the builder's position on that project; no builder scoring, no company-wide solvency assessment, no payment surveillance across a builder's book. Access and mandate: builders adopt IRIS first on their own jobs; a precondition on payment claims is void (BRB Modular v AWX [2015] QSC 218), so the duty stands on its own; facility-agreement undertakings bind the borrower and the side deed carries reporting and access rights; the legal opinion on this pathway is a month-9 milestone. Accuracy and reliance: deterministic rules over the canonical record, each figure source-linked, tested against the builder's own cost reports and the three historic record sets; confidence shown per line; professional indemnity cover sized for lender use and reliance terms agreed with the design-partner lender before any lender relies on it; no accuracy or security certification is claimed today. Competition: platforms report to their own customer and see one system; QS firms and restructuring firms are partners and channels; the defensible position is the cross-system reconciliation plus a standard lenders rely on. Pricing conflicts: a fixed fee per recovery engagement, not a share of recovery; facility fee paid by the developer as a project cost, not by the builder whose records are read. Timing: if the first facility slips to month 18 the seed is needed by month 15.
