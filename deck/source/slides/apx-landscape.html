A7

Sixty products and firms reviewed in October 2026 across eight categories: builder project-management platforms (Procore, Oracle Aconex, Autodesk Construction Cloud, Trimble Viewpoint, CMiC, Buildertrend, Fieldwire); builder ERP and cost systems (Jobpac, Cheops, Workbench, Premier, Buildxact, Simpro, Lentune); trade payment and compliance (Payapps, IPEX, Earlytrade, Textura, GCPay, Queensland project trust accounts); lender-side draw and monitoring tools (Built, Rabbet, Land Gorilla, Northspyre, nCino, Abrigo); owner project controls (Mastt, Kahua, InEight, Primavera, Asite, Aphex); AI on construction records (Datagrid, Document Crunch, Trunk Tools, Alloovium, Octant, Slate, nPlan, Buildots, Doxel, OpenSpace, ALICE, Briq); credit and counterparty data (CreditorWatch, Equifax iCIRT, illion, Dun and Bradstreet); and the service incumbents (the lender's QS firms; KordaMentha, McGrathNicol, FTI, Deloitte, KPMG). Three flags were tested for each: does it reconcile across source systems; does it give the lender a project-level position; is it written into facility documents. Only the lender's QS certificate meets the third as established practice; IPEX's vendor claims the third for some lenders; Built, Rabbet and Land Gorilla are required operationally by US lenders with no facility wording found. Cross-system reconciliation exists builder-side (Datagrid, Slate, Trunk Tools, Alloovium, Briq) and lender-side on submitted packages (Built); none combines both and reports outward to builder, developer and lender. Limits: public sources only; in-house tools at Tier 1 builders or lenders cannot be seen; IPEX's own site was not readable in the review session, so its material is as reported in sponsored articles. The dated sources are in the repository's research register.
