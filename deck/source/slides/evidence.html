Real case studies with indicative data. Names, values and delivery models altered.

Project A · $45M mixed-use

Open-book builder-developer; private credit facility arrived mid-build

$4.4M

The ledger showed $4.4M more cost than the lender’s pack, at a third complete, plus a $1.5M delay claim not yet submitted and $5.8M unlet. The lender saw it about nine months later. It decided whether the facility was in balance.

Project B · $300M residential tower

Major builder; refinance at two-thirds complete

$0

The delay-incentive pool was spent to the dollar against the forecast delay, with no weather buffer, on a $110M contract carrying $35M of change. The lender was a monthly pack behind, more than 30 times. It decided the refinance terms.

Project C · $15M commercial

Investor syndicate; private financier; QS-gated drawdowns

12 days

Delay contingency was fully used at three-quarters of the programme. Claim to cash took 12 days; the position was assembled by hand each month. It decided whether to extend the facility.

Evidence of the problem, not of IRIS. This round rebuilds the position on these files.

07

Three private record sets, held by the founders and read in full: a builder's share drive (cost-system exports, registers, claims, programmes, cashflows, PCG reports, the lender's QS initial report and the bank's term sheet); a developer's PCG pack and its capital partner's feasibility; a lender's QS drawdown report, payment certificates, PCG to the financier and trust investor report. Figures, contract sizes, delivery-model labels and lags on the slide are altered so the projects cannot be identified; the structure of each issue is as found. Project A is a builder-developer job where the builder's internal forecast ran ahead of the figure in the developer's PCG pack for about a year, with a large unsubmitted delay claim, unlet finishes trades forecast flat at budget, and a programme sliding month on month. Project B is a major-builder tower under a well-run monthly PCG whose delay-incentive pool had been consumed to the dollar by approved wet-weather days, with the bank refinancing in at about three-quarters complete. Project C is a small commercial job across two contracts under a private financier with QS-gated drawdowns across two contracts, where the delay contingency was fully used with a fifth of the programme to run, and the monthly dashboard was typed by hand from the builder's PDF, with formula errors found in the working files. For a reader outside construction: in each case the number that decided whether the lender's money was safe existed in a record before it existed in a report. These are illustrative examples of the problem; they are not outcomes IRIS has delivered.
