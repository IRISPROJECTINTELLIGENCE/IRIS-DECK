On three real jobs, the lender was months behind the records.

Real case studies with indicative data. Names, values and delivery models altered.

Project A · $45M mixed-use

More cost in the builder’s ledger than in the lender’s pack, at a third complete. The lender saw it about nine months later, while deciding whether the facility was in balance.

Project B · $300M residential tower

$0

Delay buffer left on a $110M contract carrying $35M of change. The lender was a monthly pack behind, more than thirty times, when it set the refinance terms.

12 days

From claim to cash, with the delay contingency gone at three-quarters of the programme. The lender’s position was typed by hand from the builder’s PDF each month.

Evidence of the problem, not of IRIS. This round rebuilds the position on these files.

Three private record sets, held by the founders and read in full: a builder's share drive (cost-system exports, registers, claims, programmes, cashflows, PCG reports, the lender's QS initial report and the bank's term sheet); a developer's PCG pack and its capital partner's feasibility; a lender's QS drawdown report, payment certificates, PCG to the financier and trust investor report. Figures, contract sizes, delivery-model labels and lags are altered so the projects cannot be identified; the structure of each issue is as found. Project A: the builder's internal forecast ran ahead of the figure in the developer's PCG pack for about a year, with a $1.5M delay claim not yet submitted and $5.8M of finishes trades unlet. Project B: a delay-incentive pool consumed to the dollar by approved wet-weather days, with the bank refinancing in at about three-quarters complete. Project C: a small commercial job under a private financier with QS-gated drawdowns, delay contingency fully used with a fifth of the programme to run, the monthly dashboard typed by hand with formula errors in the working files. These are illustrative examples of the problem; they are not outcomes IRIS has delivered.
