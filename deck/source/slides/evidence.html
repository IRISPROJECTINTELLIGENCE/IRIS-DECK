Real case studies with indicative data. Names, values and delivery models altered.

Project A · $45M mixed-use

Builder-developer; private credit mid-build

$4.4M

More cost in the ledger than in the lender’s pack, at a third complete. The lender saw it about nine months later, when deciding whether the facility was in balance.

Project B · $300M residential tower

Major builder; refinance at two-thirds complete

$0

Delay buffer left on a $110M contract carrying $35M of change. The lender was a monthly pack behind, more than 30 times, when it set the refinance terms.

Project C · $15M commercial

Investor syndicate; QS-gated drawdowns

12 days

From claim to cash, with the delay contingency gone at three-quarters of the programme. The position was typed by hand each month.

Evidence of the problem, not of IRIS. This round rebuilds the position on these files.

Three private record sets, held by the founders and read in full: a builder's share drive (cost-system exports, registers, claims, programmes, cashflows, PCG reports, the lender's QS initial report and the bank's term sheet); a developer's PCG pack and its capital partner's feasibility; a lender's QS drawdown report, payment certificates, PCG to the financier and trust investor report. Figures, contract sizes, delivery-model labels and lags on the slide are altered so the projects cannot be identified; the structure of each issue is as found. Project A: the builder's internal forecast ran ahead of the figure in the developer's PCG pack for about a year, with a $1.5M delay claim not yet submitted, $5.8M of finishes trades unlet and forecast flat at budget, and a programme sliding month on month. Project B: a major-builder tower under a well-run monthly PCG whose delay-incentive pool had been consumed to the dollar by approved wet-weather days, with the bank refinancing in at about three-quarters complete. Project C: a small commercial job under a private financier with QS-gated drawdowns across two contracts, where the delay contingency was fully used with a fifth of the programme to run, and the monthly dashboard was typed by hand from the builder's PDF, with formula errors in the working files. These are illustrative examples of the problem; they are not outcomes IRIS has delivered.
