A11

Backs the status line on slide 2. Built: the proof of concept on a populated Procore sandbox: a fictional project with constructed records, reconciled into positions across six domains, with findings that open to their records and a chat prototype. It has not been connected to a live project and no accuracy figure is claimed for it (slide 7). Agreed: an R&D partnership with a South East Queensland Tier 2 builder, giving access to real project data in return for the use of IRIS on its projects; the agreement and its data-processing terms are being finalised. In negotiation: an engineering partner to build the enterprise product; the statement of work is being prepared, and the $320K build line in the use of funds is an allowance until the partner’s quote. Feedback in writing: at an advisor’s request, a senior contracts manager at a Tier 1 builder reviewed the product summary in May 2026. He named three uses he would value: finding risk in correspondence, subcontractor claims and payment applications; identifying costs the budget or client revenue does not cover, which today depend on a contract administrator recording them by hand, so that a missed one leaves exposure outside the accounting system that senior management does not see; and finding delay events across the project systems that need a notice before a time bar applies, then prompting the extension-of-time notices that follow. His view was conditional on the product doing what it sets out to do. The second and third are pass marks on A14. In discussion: a non-bank private credit fund has said the lender view would be useful to it (founder-reported, D-36); there is no letter or commitment. Targets: two paying builders from month 6; IRIS for Lenders on a live facility at month 9; the first drawdown condition at month 12. Not claimed: paying customers, a lender mandate, completed assessments, an accuracy figure or a security certificate.
