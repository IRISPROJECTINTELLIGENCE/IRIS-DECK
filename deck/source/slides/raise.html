What the money proves · and the evidence each step leaves

Months 1–3

Two builder pilots live by API; a design-partner lender engaged. Evidence: signed pilot agreements; the partner’s letter.

Month 4

Position rebuilt on three historic record sets, scored. Evidence: the scored comparison against the record sets.

Month 6

Reconciles to builders’ cost reports; security certified. Evidence: the comparison with live cost reports, signed by each builder; the certificate.

Months 6–9

The design partner using the lender view on a live facility. Evidence: the partner’s written review, beside its QS report.

Month 9

PI cover, reliance terms, legal opinion on the mandate pathway. Evidence: the policy, the terms, the opinion.

Month 12

Paid builder projects; path to a first condition; assessments scoped. Evidence: paid invoices; a drafted condition with one lender; assessment scope and fee.

Months 15–18

Seed raise on that evidence, with or without a lender mandate

Milestones are framed as evidence created, not months elapsed, so the round has a purpose even if the first lender condition slips: by month 12 the company should hold live API access on real jobs, a signed comparison against builders' own cost reports and the three historic record sets, a lender design partner using the lender view on a live facility beside its QS, security certification, professional indemnity and reliance terms, and paid builder projects; the distressed-project assessment offer is scoped, not sold, inside the round. Each milestone names the artefact it leaves, so progress can be checked against documents rather than statements: signed pilot agreements and the design partner's letter (months 1–3); the comparison in two parts, first against the three historic record sets, then against the pilot builders' own cost reports with each builder signing it (months 4 and 6); the security certificate under the standard the owner selects; the design partner's written review of the lender view beside its QS report (months 6–9); the PI policy, reliance terms and legal opinion (month 9); paid invoices, a drafted drawdown condition with one lender and a scoped assessment offer with a fee (month 12). The terms are on the cover, slide 2 and here: $500K on a SAFE at a $5M cap (owner decision, 30 October 2026; previously $750K at $7.5M). The use of funds sums to $500K and matches Year 1 costs in the workbook: founders at $100K each plus 12% super ($224K), $170K of contract engineering, $30K security certification, $16K cloud and models, $20K sales, $20K legal and IP, $20K contingency including $10K insurance. The split is Claude's proposal pending the owner's confirmation. The first drawdown condition at month 12 is the base-case assumption in the model, not a commitment; the seed in months 15–18 is raised on the evidence listed, with the mandate as upside.
