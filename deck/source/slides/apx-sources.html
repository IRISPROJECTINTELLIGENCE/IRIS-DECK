Backs the dated figures on slides 2, 3 and 8. Sources are linked where the address names no lender or fund; the full register, with each address, is research/source-register.md. Exposure and loss: a sum owed to lenders is exposure, money lent and at risk; losses are set as receivers sell and funds revalue. The losses recognised so far are write-downs: three listed credit funds wrote down about $168M, 4.7% of their combined net assets, before trading was halted on 28 September 2026 (The Urban Developer, 30 September 2026); quotation was reinstated in October (CFS notice, 7 October 2026). One lender’s senior Bathla loan was sold for about $31.2M and a receiver appointed to settle presales (ABC News, 17 October 2026). Construction insolvencies: ASIC insolvency statistics, series 1, published 14 September 2026: 3,472 of 14,153 companies entering external administration in FY2025-26, 24.5%; 37.4% in July and August 2026 with the Bathla entities and 23.5% without (via The Good Builder, 22 September 2026). Bathla: 540 entities into administration on 25 August 2026 (ASIC data, via The Good Builder); about A$3.3bn owed (AFR; others report $3.4bn) and about 40 funds exposed (Bloomberg), both as reported by Alternative Credit Investor, 1 October 2026; about $3.08bn owed to secured lenders within about $3.4bn of total creditor debt, sites closed and receivers appointed (administrator’s updates, The Urban Developer, 25 September 2026). Slide 3: a lender of about ten years exited its exposure by January 2026, refinancing its remaining projects to about 12 other lenders from late 2024 (The Urban Developer, 25 August 2026); the group entered administration seven months later. A rating of one project shown on an earlier version of slide 3 was not found in open sources and has been taken off (O-25). Rates and housing: RBA cash rate to 4.85% on 3 November 2026 after five rises from 3.60% (RBA media release 26-30); ABS monthly CPI, 4.9% to October, published 25 November 2026; Cotality national index for October, published 2 November 2026, 6.7% below the March peak. Regulation: ASIC REP 814, 820 and 823 (2025); the 52-fund review of $76bn before 30 June 2026 and the Commissioner’s warning of 22 September (Clayton Utz, 1 October 2026); ASIC’s statement of 30 September naming residential construction (The Nightly, 30 September 2026). Valuation: on 18 June 2026 ASIC told private credit funds to refresh 30 June valuations and not to wait for a formal default before reassessing values; FSC Standard No. 30, Private Markets Best Practice Principles, issued 26 August 2026 and in force from 1 July 2027, requires valuation policies with independence and effective challenge, and its guidance note puts good practice at valuing at least quarterly in most circumstances. Lending terms: Stamford Capital Real Estate Debt Capital Markets Survey 2026 (March and April 2026); Gravity Capital Australian Private Credit Index, September quarter 2026, published 25 October 2026. Founder experience, labelled as such: the replacement-builder premium of 20 to 40%, and the three project examples on slide 5, which come from private records with figures altered so the jobs cannot be identified. Market and model inputs are on A5 and A6; competitor sources on A8.
