Procore and Aconex are project management platforms and overlap heavily; a project may run one or both. Procore’s core tools cover drawings and documents, RFIs and submittals, site diaries and photos, inspections and defects, meetings and programme, and, in its financial tools, budgets, subcontract commitments and variations. Aconex, owned by Oracle, grew out of document control shared by the client, consultants and builder: a controlled document register with version control, formal mail kept as a permanent record, review and approval workflows and an audit trail that can’t be altered, with field, cost and model tools added since. Jobpac is the builder’s ERP: project costing, budgeting, forecasting, payroll and contract administration. PayApps handles subcontractors’ payment claims, certification and payment. ProcurePro runs tendering and trade letting, and holds the subcontracts. Email and Office carry the day-to-day correspondence across all parties, and the spreadsheets where much of the monthly reconciliation is done. Monthly reporting covers the builder’s internal cost reporting and the PCG report to the client; the builder’s own team assembles both. The lender’s QS does substantiate each progress claim, and that matters: it values work in place from a site inspection and checks the claim against the contract sum. But its inputs are the claim, the builder’s variation and EOT registers as submitted, the statutory declaration that trades are paid, and the builder’s programme; it reports that this information was not independently verified, makes no allowance for defects, and does not see the cost ledger, the unsubmitted changes or the notices beneath the claim. On the three jobs we read, each figure the QS certified equalled what the builder claimed.
