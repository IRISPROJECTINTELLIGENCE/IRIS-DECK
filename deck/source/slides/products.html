The products

07

One platform. Two products.
Each party sees what it is entitled to see.

IRIS for Lenders

The position beneath the certificate, continuous, for each facility on the book.

Written into the facility as a drawdown condition, as the QS is.

In distress, the same position in days, for the lender, its receiver or administrator.

Paid by the developer · 15 bps a year on the facility

IRIS for Builders

The same position, first. Notices near their time bar, unlet scope, trades owed, defects against completion.

The builder keeps its margin and is seen fairly by the people funding it.

Its lender gets a reconciled position rather than a month of questions.

Paid by the builder · $3,500 a month per live job

Role-based permissions. The builder authorises the connection and sees each finding first. The lender sees the reconciled position, with drill-down to the record, and does not see the builder’s raw systems. The developer sees what its lender sees.

One platform with role-based permissions (D-35). The builder's product and the lender's product read the same reconciled position; what differs is the view, the entitlement and the buyer. The builder authorises the read-only connection, sees every finding first and can annotate it; commercially sensitive items stay with the builder. The lender sees the reconciled position and the record behind each figure, not the builder's systems. The developer sees the lender view for its own facility. Distressed-project assessment is proposed functionality at a separate fee, offered after the platform is proven on live jobs; no engagement has been run (appendix A12). Hypothesis, labelled: a monitored facility can price inside an unmonitored one, so the developer's fee is paid out of a financing-cost saving; the design partner is asked at month 9.
