A14

Backs slides 7 and 13. Proposed by the company as the pass marks for the R&D pilot, to be agreed with the builder before the pilot starts; none has been measured yet. Agreement to source: forecast final cost, cost to date, committed cost and revenue claimed agree to the builder’s month-end cost report for three months running, with each difference explained (timing, accruals, manual journals). Traceability: each figure and each finding opens the source record it rests on, checked by sample. Precision: at least eight in ten of the findings IRIS raises are confirmed as real by the builder’s commercial team. Recall: at least nine in ten of the items in the builder’s own risk and opportunity register are found; items IRIS finds that the register does not hold are counted separately, as value. Lead time: the median finding appears at least 30 days before it shows in the builder’s monthly cost report or PCG report, measured on the live jobs and back-tested on the three historic record sets on slide 5, where the outcome is known. Time bars: each delay event in the records is flagged before its notice deadline under the contract; this is one of the uses a Tier 1 senior contracts manager named in writing (A11). Commercial value: exposure the project team did not already hold, valued and confirmed by the builder, worth at least ten times the subscription, about $420,000 a job a year; this is his second use, cost the budget does not cover that is recorded by hand or missed. Use: the project team uses IRIS unaided for a full monthly reporting cycle, the exit test of the first release. Conversion: the builder pays from month 6. The lender equivalent follows at month 9: the design partner reads the lender view beside its QS report for three months and records which figures it would use in a drawdown decision. When models start reading documents (R3), extraction accuracy is measured against a held-out set.
