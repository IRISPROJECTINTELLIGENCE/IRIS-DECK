A5

Backs the market tiles on slide 11. Each step is a line on the Market sheet of the workbook, and each input is on the Inputs sheet with its source and type. Measured or reported: the IBISWorld segment revenues (E3019: medium-rise $18.9bn plus high-rise $14.6bn; E3021, commercial and industrial building: $70.9bn), the major-market shares (developers and REITs fund 88% of multi-unit and 73% of commercial work) and the state shares (Queensland 19.6% of multi-unit work done and 18.2% of commercial establishments); the ABS apartment completion time of 29 months. Estimates: the 65% share of commercial work on jobs of $10M or more, the 24-month commercial build period, and the debt stack at about 1.2 times the build contract. The market counts live jobs at a $40M average contract, which makes the average facility across the market $48M (D-52). The facilities IRIS is written into are larger, $80M in the base case (A6, D-50), because the lenders that adopt first write the mid- and high-rise jobs; that sets the lender line, not the size of the market. Cross-checks on job size: the top 50 builders started 722 projects worth $43.9bn in 2025, about $61M each (Hubexo); Brisbane and Gold Coast apartment projects on hold average about $48M (PRD, September 2026); a listed lender’s senior construction loans average about $39M (June 2026). Revenue potential: 3,470 developer-funded jobs at $72,000 (15 bps on $48M), 860 other jobs at $42,000, plus assessments at 4% of funded jobs a year at $35,000. Queensland: 660 facilities and 160 builder jobs; South East Queensland is about 79% of the state’s building pipeline (CSQ, 2026). Out of scope: low-rise apartments, townhouses and house building, which are mostly jobs under $10M, and public work, which carries no construction facility. Not counted: insurers and surety providers, who hold about $8.7bn of security on these jobs.
