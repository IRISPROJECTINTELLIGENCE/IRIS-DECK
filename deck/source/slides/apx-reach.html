A4

Backs slide 10. Why each party signs. The lender lends against cost to finish and the completion date, and today reads both from the QS report and the builder’s own report each month; IRIS gives it the same figures from the records as they move, with the record behind each one. That a lender values this enough to write it into a facility is the hypothesis the design partner tests. The developer already pays for lender-required monitoring, the QS report, as a condition of its debt; IRIS is paid the same way, at 15 bps a year. That a lender prices a facility it can see into at a lower rate is a hypothesis put to the design partner (D-46); no lender has confirmed it. The builder: on a facility with the condition, IRIS is a condition of the tender for all bidders, so no bidder is put at a disadvantage, and the builder sees each finding before the lender does. Whether builders accept it is untested until a lender adopts. What binds whom. The facility agreement binds the borrower, as a condition of drawdown. The builder is bound by what it signs: the tender and award conditions, a head contract clause giving read-only API access to that project’s systems for monitoring, and the side deed (tripartite deed) signed by lender, developer and builder at financial close, which lenders already require. A builder already under contract owes nothing new; the first conditions go on new facilities, extensions and refinances. The builder’s duty stands on its own and is not a precondition to its payment claims: a contractual precondition to payment claims was held void in BRB Modular v AWX [2015] QSC 218. The drawdown condition is met by an exported pack, so a builder cannot hold up the developer’s drawdown. Where interests pull apart, and how IRIS handles it. Lender and builder: the lender wants detail; the builder wants its margin and trade prices kept. Proposed rule (O-20): the lender and developer see totals, movement and the records behind them; trade letting and rates stay in the builder’s view unless cost to finish exceeds what is left in the contract, or the lender steps in under its deed. Subcontract confidentiality terms decide what trade data can be shown. Developer and lender: the developer pays and the lender relies. IRIS is paid a fixed fee per facility with no share of any outcome; it reports what the builder’s system of record shows, and where the builder’s internal and developer-facing forecasts differ it reports the system of record and flags the gap without adjudicating. Builder first (proposed; not yet in the product requirements, O-21): the builder sees each finding first and can add its explanation before the lender view refreshes; the finding and the explanation then reach the lender together, and the history is kept. One job, one fee: a job under a lender’s condition pays the facility fee, and the builder’s subscription applies to its other jobs. Assessments are a fixed fee per engagement, with no stake in the recovery. Reliance and data permissions are on A13. The drafting is confirmed with lawyers at the month-9 opinion; this is not legal advice.
