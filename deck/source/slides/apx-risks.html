A10

Backs the milestones on slide 13. Six risks, each with the action and the point at which we will know. The first two are the ones the round is built to answer: lender adoption is unproven, and builder access rests so far on one R&D partnership. Lender: a design-partner lender is engaged in months 1 to 3 and shapes the view; IRIS runs on a live facility beside its QS at month 9; the first drawdown condition at month 12 is a company target, and the seed at months 15 to 18 is raised on a product in paid use with or without it. Builders: the builder gets IRIS’s view of its own job first, nothing is written back and rates stay with it. Connections: builder-authorised API connections, one adapter per system, built system by system, with a register per project of what it runs; Procore first. Reliance: IRIS is reconciled to builders’ own cost reports and to three historic record sets before a lender relies on it; professional indemnity cover is sized for lender use and reliance terms are set with the design partner; IRIS certifies nothing, and no accuracy figure or security certification is claimed today. Competition: platforms report to their own customer, and requiring one would dictate the builder’s software; QS and restructuring firms could add the capability and are as likely to be channels as rivals; Procore with Datagrid is the platform to watch (slide 9). Sales cycles: lender procurement, panels and legal review are slow; if the first facility slips to month 18 the seed is needed by month 15. Two further risks sit with assessments on A9: access after an appointment, and pricing, which is a fixed fee so that IRIS has no stake in the recovery it reports on.
