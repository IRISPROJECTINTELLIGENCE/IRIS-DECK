A3 · The risks we see, and what we’re doing about them.

Risk

What we’re doing · when we’ll know

No lender adds the condition

Shape it with a design-partner lender from month 3; review a live facility before asking for a condition. If none will, the builder line stands; the seed isn’t raised on the mandate. Months 9–12.

Builders resist or opt out; access not granted

The builder gets IRIS’s view of its own job first; nothing written back; rates stay with it. Live access is negotiated, not assumed; the historic record sets carry the reconciliation until it lands. Months 1–3.

Data access and integration

Builder-authorised API connections that write nothing back; one adapter per system API, built system by system, with a register per project of what it runs. Procore first; the rest by month 6. Months 1–6.

Reconciliation, reliance and professional indemnity

Reconciled to builders’ own cost reports and three historic sets before any lender relies on it; PI sized for lender use; reliance terms with the design partner. IRIS certifies nothing. Months 4–9.

Recovery access, permissions and missing records

Access rests on facility rights, the side deed, an appointee’s powers or the developer’s consent, under confidentiality terms; not automatic after an appointment. Gaps reported as gaps. Legal opinion. Month 9.

A platform, QS firm or restructuring firm builds it

Platforms report to their own customer; requiring one would dictate the builder’s software. QS and restructuring firms could add it; at a lender’s scale it’s software, and both are as likely channels as rivals. Ongoing.

Validation and sales cycles run long

Lender procurement, panels and legal review are slow. Conservative case: first facility at month 18, builders at half the rate. Milestones are evidence created, so a late mandate doesn’t void the round. Month 12.

Conflicts in recovery pricing

A fee tied to the recovery would give IRIS a stake in what it reports, so assessments are a fixed fee per engagement, set with the first buyer. The facility fee is paid by the developer, not the builder. First engagement.

Eight risks, each with the action and the point at which we will know. The first two are the ones the round is built to answer: builder access and lender adoption are both unproven, and neither is assumed in the milestones on slide 17. Accuracy and reliance: no accuracy figure or security certification is claimed today; reconciliation to the builders' cost reports at month 6 and certification are milestones. No accuracy percentage, protocol or tolerance is stated, at the owner's direction; the milestone is that the position reconciles to the builders' own cost reports. Procore, with Datagrid and its owner tools, is the platform to watch (slide 13). Recovery: proposed functionality; the legal pathway to a troubled project's records differs before and after an appointment and is the subject of the month-9 legal opinion. Not listed as a risk: the cycle turning, since the layer goes unreported in good markets too and facility fees grow with lending. The Queensland Productivity Commission found project trust accounts have not effectively reduced non-payment (reported); they do not give the lender a view.
