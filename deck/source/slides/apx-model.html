A6

Backs the base case on slide 11. The workbook runs five years in three cases; each row on this page is on its Inputs or Summary sheet. Lender acquisition: lenders adopt at months 12, 18, 22, 26, 29, 32, 34 and 36, then about six a year to 20 at month 60, a company target. Lender 1 is the design partner; lenders 2 onwards follow a live result there. The market is concentrated: real estate private credit in Australia is about $80bn, 31% of residential development debt, and the five largest providers hold 52% of it (CBRE, 15 November 2026), so a handful of adoptions carries most of the volume. The number of active construction lenders is not published. Facility conversion: each lender adds 0.8 new facilities a month, about ten a year, which at $80M is about $770M of new construction facilities a year, a book of about $1.5bn on two-year terms; plus ten existing loans re-signed under the condition over 18 months at extension or refinance; facilities leave after a 24-month term. The Year 5 book is $29.8bn of facility limits across twenty lenders, which assumes the larger lenders adopt first; the test is the design partner’s own origination. Pricing: 15 bps a year on the facility limit, $120,000 on $80M, above a $3,500 a month floor with no cap (D-51). For scale, non-bank senior construction facilities were priced in the high single digits to low double digits a year in 2026, with establishment and line fees of one to a few per cent of the limit (Feasly, July 2026); 15 bps is about 1.5% of a year’s interest at 10%. No lender has agreed the fee; the design partner is asked what it would pay. Retention: no lender stops adding facilities within five years (an input, D-55). A facility under the condition keeps it to completion; a lender that leaves adds no new ones. If each lender stopped after three years, Year 5 revenue would fall by $1.0M; each mature lender is about $2.2M a year of fees (about 19 facilities). Unit economics in Year 5 terms: one facility pays $120,000 a year; a deployment analyst at one per eight live jobs costs about $16,800 a job, cloud and model use $3,000, and commission (8% of the first year’s fee) with onboarding ($4,000) spread over the term $6,800; contribution $93,400, 78%. A builder job pays $42,000 and carries the same analyst and cloud cost. Operating margin, Year 5: revenue $40.5M; costs that grow with jobs $10.7M; lender success and engineering $4.3M; fixed costs (directors, fixed roles, overheads, contingency) $7.4M; EBITDA $18.1M, 45%. The analyst ratio is the largest cost lever: six jobs each takes Year 5 EBITDA to $15.8M. Revenue and run rate: Year 5 revenue is the sum of months 49 to 60. Revenue grows through the year, from $2.6M in month 49 to $4.1M in month 60, so the run rate (month × 12) is $31.8M at month 49 and $49.2M at month 60. Builder line: two pilots pay from month 6, then a new builder at intervals of four to eight months, each starting with two jobs and growing to six, at $3,500 a month a job. Assessments: from month 30, $35,000 each, benchmarked to QS report fees. Results: revenue $0.19M / $2.3M / $9.3M / $22.7M / $40.5M; EBITDA −$0.48M / −$0.31M / $2.4M / $8.9M / $18.1M; upside $78M and conservative $6.2M in Year 5. Downside cases on A15.
