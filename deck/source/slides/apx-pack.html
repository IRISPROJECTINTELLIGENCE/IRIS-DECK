A1

Backs slide 6, which says the lender view is part of this round’s build. The page shows what that view is for: five decisions a lender makes on a funded project, what each is made on today and what IRIS adds. Funds left to finish: lenders call this keeping the loan in balance, cost to complete against undrawn funds and equity. The QS assesses cost to complete on the footing that the original builder finishes for the contract price (AIQS guidance note, 2019); IRIS adds the builder’s own forecast and what is moving it, such as variations not yet priced, delays not yet notified and trades let against allowances. Completion date: presale settlements repay the loan, and completion past a sunset date lets purchasers rescind. Notices matter because entitlement to time and money depends on notices given in time; a missed notice turns a recoverable delay into the builder’s own cost. Builder failure: what the lender recovers is roughly the completed value, less the cost to finish and the cost of delay; the assessment service is on A9. Scope: IRIS reads the funded project and makes no solvency call on the builder as a company. A builder short of cash tends to show it on the project first, as trades paid late or packages left unlet. Status: planned capability. The design-partner lender decides what the view shows and how it is laid out; the milestone is IRIS for Lenders on a live facility beside the lender’s QS at month 9 (slide 13). Each line on the view links to the source records behind it, and a pack can be exported at each drawdown to sit beside the QS report.
