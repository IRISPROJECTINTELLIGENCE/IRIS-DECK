A1

An illustrative lender view, ranked by impact on the lender's position. 01 Facility in balance is the lender's core test: cost to complete, plus exposure the systems show but the claims don't yet carry, against undrawn funds and equity. 02 Presale settlements repay the loan; completion past a sunset date lets purchasers rescind. 03 The builder's forecast final cost against the contract sum is its margin on the job; a forecast loss is an early sign the job is under stress. 04 Time bars rank fourth because entitlement is derived from notices: a missed notice turns a recoverable delay or variation into the builder's own cost, with liquidated damages on top, so the rights expiring in the next 30 and 60 days are where the position is made or lost. 05 Trades owed against claimed and paid, and project trust shortfalls, show the builder's trouble reaching the site, often first as trades paid late. 06 Quality: open defects, rework and non-conformances decide whether practical completion is reached on the programme and what it costs to get there; lenders and their QS have no allowance for rectification, so this is exposure the certificate does not carry. 07 Security and cover: retentions and bank security held against work certified, and insurance and licences in date, are what the lender recovers against if the builder stops; a lapse is direct, uninsured exposure. 08 Claims and disputes: trades' claims, adjudications and disputes in train are cost and delay the builder carries before its forecast shows them, and the fastest route to a stopped site. 09 and 10: unlet scope and unsubmitted variations are cost-to-complete risk that line 01 already counts; they sit lower because they are inputs to it, not separate exposures. Grey, amber and coral are illustrative states. Each line links to the source records that compute it; at each drawdown the lender exports a pack to sit beside the QS report.
