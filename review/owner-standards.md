# Owner standards for the deck

Every criticism the project owner has made of the deck between 24 October and 4 November 2026, turned into a checklist. A reviewer applies every item to every slide before the owner sees a version. An item fails if a reasonable reader would say the criticism applies.

## Language

1. **Banned words** on any slide (notes excluded): nobody, every, only (except in "read-only"), never, always, guarantees, prevents, real time. Grep before publishing.
2. **No "this, not that" framing** ("revenue potential, not spend"; "a summary, not the records"). State the thing plainly.
3. **No stacked fragments in threes** ("Builders prove it. A lender shapes it. The mandate scales it."). The owner called this the AI register. One idea per sentence, sentences of varied length, written the way someone would say it across a table.
4. **No insider shorthand without saying what it is**: "the position", "source-linked", "the certificate", "hand-off", "the wedge". A first-time investor must be able to follow without a construction background.
5. **Headings make the point.** The 30 October headings were preferred to later rewrites. A heading states the slide's conclusion in plain words; the second line, where there is one, adds the consequence.
6. **Executive, not naive.** Confident, specific, sourced. No adjectives doing the work of evidence.
7. **Each sentence makes a point.** Cut to the crux. Words do not carry nuance the reader has to decode.
8. **Clear to someone reading it for the first time.** Not blurry, not context-overloaded.

## Structure

9. **Each slide carries one point, and that point ties to the next slide.** The deck is one argument, not twelve explanations.
10. **The problem and the current mechanism are understood before the solution appears**, so the solution's value and its place in the ecosystem are obvious.
11. **The overarching mechanism, not every mechanism.** Explain how the value is added at each level; do not teach contracting detail. Detail goes to the appendix.
12. **The three parties and the distressed case are mapped**: builder, developer, lender, and what happens in distress, with who pays, who sees what, and why each one wants it.
13. **Builders are the wedge; lenders are the scale.** The developer may finance on better terms because of the mandate. All four value propositions appear.
14. **Market size is TAM, SAM, SOM**, built from each revenue stream and rolled into one revenue, cost and profitability model.
15. **The competitive analysis is real**: named products, what each reads and serves, where it stops, with sources. Investors need guidance through the noise, not a claim that nothing exists.
16. **No new slides unless something is consolidated.**

## Evidence

17. **Every number has a basis** shown or in the notes: verified fact, founder observation (labelled), illustrative example, modelled assumption, demonstrated MVP capability, planned capability, or hypothesis (labelled).
18. **No invented fees, volumes or outcomes.** Assessment fees are benchmarked. Lender adoption is labelled a target.
19. **No prospect, lender, fund, project or employer names.** The three record sets stay unidentifiable. No "accuracy test" claims. No reliance-letter mechanism. API-only connection.
20. **The revenue model is explosive and defensible**: the curve comes from lenders bringing their books, every input on the Inputs sheet with its source, three cases.

## Design

21. **Full slides.** No large empty areas; the 808px content budget is used.
22. **Not text-dense.** Every slide is built around a table, chart, diagram or figures, with short text around it. Nothing the reader has to cognitively process to extract.
23. **Low visual weight.** The slide should feel like relief, not load. One central element.
24. **Same-size headings**, consistent labels, consistent spacing; the design language of the 30 October deck (cream, ink, coral, Inter).
25. **Appendix separate.** Detail lives in the appendix deck; the main deck does not carry it.

## Terms and sequence (do not change without a decision)
