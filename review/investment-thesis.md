# Investment thesis

Status: approved baseline wording as of 29 October 2026. Changes require a numbered decision in `review-decisions.md`.

## The thesis in one line

IRIS gives construction capital a source-linked view of project delivery risk, reconciling fragmented project records to identify emerging exposure and inform funding, intervention and recovery decisions.

## The problem

Construction produces about one in four Australian corporate insolvencies (ASIC, FY2025–26). Most are small builders; the money problem sits on the funded jobs among them. When a funded project gets into trouble, the first question is what its real position is, and that answer sits in records no one party holds, reconciled by hand, monthly, and reaches the people funding the job late, in part, or not at all. The lender's monthly check is a QS certificate on the head contract, built from what the builder submits; the project's true risk position (entitlement at the time bar, forecast movement not yet notified, contingency and liquid allowances, market exposure in the trades, completion risk, validity of what is claimed) sits beneath it in the builder's systems.

## What IRIS does that existing processes do not

Reads the builder's systems by read-only API, reconciles them across systems into one position, links each figure to its record, flags gaps and conflicts between records, and shows the same position to builder, developer and lender through permissioned views. The QS, the site inspection and professional judgement stay. No system found replicates this as of October 2026; adjacent software is a source IRIS reads or a tool it sits beside.

## Three applications of one platform, in the order they can be proven

1. **Builder pilot deployments (now).** Live projects, $3,500 a month per project after an R&D period. Gate: the position reconciles to the builder's own cost reports and finds exposure the builder had not priced; an accuracy report.
2. **Lender design partner (alongside).** A non-bank private credit fund shapes the lender view from the pilots: what to show, how it is structured, how it bears on the facility and the lender's position. Gate: the partner uses it on a live facility beside its QS; security certification, professional indemnity and reliance terms in place.
3. **Monitoring at scale, then assessments.** A drawdown condition on new facilities, extensions and refinances, paid by the developer at 15 bps a year on the facility limit (floor $3,500, cap $10,000 a month); then other lenders after a live result. Distressed-project assessments follow at a separate fixed fee per engagement, as a later application on the same position, not the entry point.

Nothing is assumed automatic: no access, no pilot; no reconciled result, no lender test; no live result, no condition.

## Why now (market-led; AI is the enabler, not the reason)

Cash rate up 100 bps in 2026 to 4.60% (30 September); a $3.08bn secured-lender exposure when a developer-builder group entered administration on 25 August 2026, with about 40 private credit funds exposed, redemptions paused and receivers appointed; ASIC's 52-fund valuation review, "prepare for enforcement" (22 September) and a 30 September statement naming residential construction; three listed credit funds suspended on 28 September after about $168M was written down; a $2.3bn real-estate credit fund suspended on 22 October. Counterargument carried on the slide: capital has not left ($1.35bn of new CRE lending mandates announced 26 October; market about $213bn).

## What the round buys

$750K SAFE at a $7.5M cap: live API access on two builder pilots; the position rebuilt and scored on three historic record sets; an accuracy report against builders' own cost reports; security certification; the design partner live on a facility beside its QS; PI cover, reliance terms and a legal opinion on the mandate pathway; paid builder projects and a path to a first condition; assessments scoped. Seed of about $3M in months 15–18 on that evidence, with or without a mandate.

## The crux

Two unproven things carry the thesis: lawful, timely access to a builder's records, and reconciliation accuracy a lender will rely on. The deck says so, and the round is framed as the instrument that tests both.
